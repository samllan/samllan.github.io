St Andrews · MSc research

Finding and counting bacteria

Make the microscopic easier to measure.

A microscope reveals a crowded, imperfect world: faint bacteria, overlapping cells and background clutter. My St Andrews research explored how computer vision could help specialists locate and count tuberculosis bacteria in fluorescence images.

Small objects. A substantial challenge.

Find the region.
Count the cells.
Let an expert look closer.

One network proposes regions of interest. A second estimates the number of bacteria inside each region. Saved crops and predictions make the intermediate decisions available for review.

Research evidence and wider ambitions

The detector used Faster R-CNN with a ResNet-50 backbone; the main counting experiments used ResNeXt-50. The work also addressed crop padding for rotation, consistent annotation, model training and experiment logging.

The thesis reports detection of over 80% of in-focus cells in a tested subset after training the detector on 100 images. This is an image-level prototype result, not patient-level diagnostic sensitivity. The separate unseen-patient counting test is reported above.

A longer-term aim was to add analysis of bacterial characteristics, including lipid content, to support research into treatment response. That classifier and clinical outcome prediction were not established by this project.

Source: Understanding Tuberculosis — Computer Vision Based Analysis of Images Acquired Using a Novel Cell Staining Method, Sam Llanwarne, MSc Advanced Computer Science, University of St Andrews, 16 August 2019. Printed pp23–34: pipeline; p37: detection; pp42–43, Table 7: unseen-patient counting; p45: future directions.

The aim

Build an inspectable image-analysis pipeline that finds likely bacterial regions and estimates how many cells they contain, with a longer-term interest in studying bacterial changes during treatment.

The work

I developed the Python/PyTorch pipeline around established detection and counting networks, prepared and labelled images with domain-expert input, built image transformations and experiment records, and investigated performance on increasingly varied data.

What exists

The submitted MSc thesis documents the pipeline, detection overlays, counting experiments and error analysis. A small test on images from a patient unseen during training produced a total count of 91 against an expert count of 106.

The useful insight

Useful scientific AI makes its workings visible: show the regions, inspect the ambiguous cases, and distinguish a close total from accurate individual predictions.
